# Encinal HRP Handbook Guide, 2026-2027

This is a task-focused digest of the [official HRP handbook](https://docs.google.com/document/d/179hXUbx30VppHvIYFaa_zpmoqHiDlo7oqeuflOZdNMQ/edit?tab=t.0), reviewed August 31, 2026. Use the official document when exact or newly updated wording matters.

## Communication and Privacy

- ParentSquare is the primary class channel. The teacher and HRP can post; the HRP may also need to post for leads such as Garden and Yearbook.
- Keep posting frequency considerate. School administration, PTO, MPAEF, and committees also communicate school-wide; HRP follow-ups should add class-specific warmth or coordination rather than duplicate everything.
- A separate class contact list is customary but optional. Obtain explicit permission before sharing a family's name, email, or phone number with the class.
- WhatsApp is optional and opt-in. Use it for community, questions, and reminders, not as a replacement for ParentSquare announcements or sign-ups.
- HRP Chair / ParentSquare help: `ptohrpchair@encinalschool.com`.

## Volunteers and Leads

- Recruit volunteers according to the teacher's needs, such as Mystery Readers, Marcy Cook math groups, field trips, recurring classroom activities, and parties. ParentSquare events can hold sign-ups and reminders.
- Every classroom volunteer or field-trip chaperone must be fingerprint-cleared under district requirements.
- Garden Lead is needed early if the class will participate; without one, the class will not receive a garden bed. The role is roughly 30-60 minutes monthly and does not require gardening experience. Once filled, connect the volunteer with `garden@encinalschool.com`.
- Every class needs a Classroom Yearbook Editor. If nobody volunteers, the HRP is responsible for ensuring class representation. Send the selected volunteer's name and contact information to Yearbook Chair Stacey Shirer at `stacey.shirer@gmail.com`.
- The Yearbook Editor gathers family and teacher photos during the year, adds class candids in TreeRing in spring, and works with the Yearbook Chair.
- When recruiting, state the actual role, time commitment, available support, deadline, and how to volunteer. Follow up after Back to School Night because not every family attends.

## Parties and Celebrations

- Expect three or four class celebrations: Halloween, Winter Holidays, Valentine's Day, and End of Year.
- For every party, first ask the teacher for the date/time, desired format, whether parents lead or drop off activities, whether to coordinate with another class or the grade, and what help is wanted.
- If parents attend and lead, at least one HRP should be present to coordinate.
- Confirm allergies and dietary restrictions with the teacher and follow district wellness guidance before planning food.
- ParentSquare can communicate the event and collect sign-ups for volunteers, snacks, paper goods, crafts, and supplies.

## Gifts and Appreciation

- Family contributions and participation are always voluntary. Do not suggest or specify an expected dollar amount.
- Every collective gift is from the entire class, regardless of who contributed. Prefer a collective class gift over encouraging direct individual contributions when organizing a group effort.
- Common occasions are Winter Break, teacher birthday, Teacher Appreciation Week, and End of Year.
- Winter and End-of-Year options include an optional pooled gift, a contributed-item gift basket, or simply sharing the teacher's favorite things so families may act individually.
- If pooling funds, the majority should generally benefit the classroom teacher. Small tokens for aides, specialists, interns, or other regular support staff may be appropriate.
- Teacher birthdays can center on student-made cards, with optional sign-ups based on verified favorite things. Do not invent the birthday or preferences.
- Support-staff birthday recognition is optional and should reflect how closely those staff members support the class.

## Teacher Appreciation Week

- Teacher Appreciation Week is May 3-7, 2027.
- The PTO committee leads the week; coordinate class participation with its schedule so extra plans do not duplicate PTO-provided treats or meals.
- Flower Day typically asks each child to bring one or two flowers for the teacher and a support staff member.
- At least two mornings may need a parent before school to collect and present items; those shifts can be delegated rather than handled only by the HRP.

## Other Community Tasks

- Share and occasionally remind families about Ms. Napolitani's classroom Book Fair wish list once the link is supplied. Book Fair contact: Aanika Carroll at `aanika.c@gmail.com`.
- Coordinate with other second-grade HRPs on an inclusive grade-level playdate before December. Include all students across programs; a simple playground or park gathering is sufficient.
- Class placemats are an optional keepsake project and may be delegated. Photos, birthdays, and contact details require careful permission handling.

## Year-at-a-Glance

- August / September: meet with teacher; agree expectations; divide co-HRP work; set up calendar and contact-list process; recruit Garden and Yearbook leads; publish early sign-ups.
- Late September: share the Halloween date if parent volunteers will be needed.
- October: plan and run the Halloween party with the teacher.
- Late November / early December: explain the optional Winter gift plan and share the Winter party date.
- Before Winter Break: plan and run the Winter party.
- Late January: ask the teacher about Valentine's Day plans.
- February: support the teacher's preferred Valentine's activities.
- Mid-April: review the PTO Teacher Appreciation schedule; plan Flower/Card Day communication and recruit helpers.
- Mid-May: communicate any optional end-of-year gift plan; begin End-of-Year party planning, possibly across the grade.
- June: organize and oversee the End-of-Year party.

## Back to School Night Checklist

Before: review ParentSquare; align with co-HRPs; ask the teacher about needs, dates, and a brief introduction slot; prepare a consent-based contact-list method; prioritize Garden and Yearbook recruitment.

During: introduce the HRP role and communication channel; recruit lead volunteers; explain contact-list permission; optionally invite families to WhatsApp; preview later volunteer opportunities.

After: follow up on ParentSquare; continue recruiting urgent roles; remind absent families about the contact-list opt-in; check in regularly with the teacher; give families as much notice as possible.
